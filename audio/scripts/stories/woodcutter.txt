The Honest Woodcutter.

Long ago, deep in a beautiful forest filled with tall oak trees and singing birds, there lived a kind woodcutter named Ahmad.
Ahmad was poor. His clothes were worn thin, his house was small, and his tools were old.
But his heart was as pure and honest as fresh spring water.
Every single day, Ahmad walked into the forest, cut wood with his plain iron axe, and sold it at the market to feed his family.
It was hard and tiring work, but Ahmad always did it with a cheerful and grateful heart.
One warm afternoon, as Ahmad was chopping wood near the edge of a shimmering blue lake, his axe slipped from his tired hands.
There was a loud splash, and it sank straight to the bottom of the deep water.
Ahmad sat down by the water and began to weep.
Without his axe, he could cut no wood. Without wood, he could earn no money. Without money, his children would go hungry.
What shall I do? he cried into his hands.
Suddenly, the surface of the lake began to ripple and glow with a soft golden light.
A shining spirit rose slowly from the water. She was the Guardian of the Lake.
She smiled gently and asked, Why do you weep so sadly, kind woodcutter?
Ahmad told her exactly what had happened.
The Guardian nodded and plunged her hand into the glittering water.
She returned holding a magnificent golden axe that gleamed like sunlight.
Is this your axe? she asked.
Ahmad's eyes went wide with wonder. It was the most beautiful tool he had ever seen.
But he slowly shook his head. No, kind spirit. That is not mine. My axe was a simple, plain iron axe.
The Guardian smiled and dipped her hand into the water again.
This time she returned with a beautiful silver axe, glittering like bright moonlight.
Is this your axe?
Ahmad again shook his head gently. No, kind spirit. That is not mine either.
The Guardian reached into the water one final time and lifted out a wet, rusty, old iron axe.
Is this your axe? she asked.
Yes! Ahmad cried with tears of relief and joy. That is my axe! Oh, thank you so very much!
The Guardian of the Lake felt deeply moved.
You are a truly honest soul, she said warmly.
Because you told the truth when you could have claimed something far more valuable, I give you all three axes as your reward.
Ahmad walked home that evening carrying three axes and tears of gratitude in his eyes.
He never told a lie, never took what was not his, and his family lived in comfort and happiness for the rest of their days.
And the lesson the forest remembered for a thousand years was simply this.
Honesty is not just the right thing to do. It is the most powerful and precious treasure of all.
The End.
