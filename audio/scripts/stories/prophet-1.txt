The Boy Who Became the Trustworthy One.

A long, long time ago, about 1,450 years ago, in a desert city called Makkah, a baby boy was born.
His name was Muhammad, peace be upon him.
His father, Abdullah, had passed away before he was born.
When he was just six years old, his mother, Aminah, also passed away.
But Allah took care of him.
First his kind grandfather looked after him, and then his loving uncle, Abu Talib.
Muhammad, peace be upon him, never went to school.
He never learned to read or write, not even his own name.
In Makkah, people loved poetry, and poets were famous for their beautiful words.
But Muhammad, peace be upon him, was not a poet, and he never made up poems.
As a boy, he looked after sheep on the hills around Makkah.
He was gentle with the animals, patient, and always helpful.
When he grew up, he became a trader and travelled with caravans of camels to faraway lands.
He never cheated and never told a lie.
People trusted him so much that they gave him a special name: Al-Amin, the Trustworthy One.
When he was forty years old, he liked to go to a quiet cave on a mountain called Hira to think and pray.
One night, the angel Jibreel came to him and said, “Iqra!”, which means “Read!”
Muhammad, peace be upon him, was frightened and said, “I cannot read.”
Jibreel said it again, and again, and then taught him the very first words of the Quran, the message from Allah.
From that day, he became the Prophet of Allah.
A man who could not read or write began reciting words so beautiful that even the greatest poets of Makkah were amazed.
They knew these words could not be his own.
They came from Allah.
He taught people to worship only one God, to be kind to their parents, to share food with the hungry, to be gentle with animals, and to always tell the truth.
Some people made life very hard for him, but he stayed patient and kind.
Later he moved to a city called Madinah, where people welcomed him with joy.
